# Day 3 review notes (रेलवे स्टेशन पर)

- Learner role `passenger` (यात्री). Other roles: `staff` (कर्मचारी), `porter` (कुली), `co_passenger` (सहयात्री). All non-learner roles are neutral: none uses a gendered form about itself.
- Scene 11 labels the speaker "रेलवे कर्मचारी"; it is mapped to the same `staff` role (label कर्मचारी). The English column calls this role "Clerk" in scenes 1–2 and "Staff" later.
- Voices follow the neutral default (female voice when the learner is a man). For the कुली a teacher may prefer a male voice; this is allowed for a neutral role and would only change `porter.voice`.
- Scene order: the source puts situation 8 (कुली) before 7 (सीट ढूँढ़ना). Scenes are in number order: d03s7 = सीट ढूँढ़ना, d03s8 = कुली से सहायता लेना.
- Stage direction "(प्लेटफ़ॉर्म पर पहुँचने के बाद)" in scene 8 is not a line; it is described in the scene's `situation`.
- d03s2: two consecutive कर्मचारी lines ("यह फार्म भर दीजिए" / "आरक्षण हो गया है") kept as in source.
- f variants (1): d03s12 "क्या मैं उसी टिकट से यात्रा कर सकती हूँ?". No f for "क्या आप मेरा सामान उठा सकते हैं?" (addressed to the porter), "क्या कुछ कम हो सकता है?" (impersonal), "सीट के पास रखा था" (agrees with the bag).
- Source Hindi to check (not changed):
  - "आपके पास कितने सामान हैं?" — सामान is uncountable; "कितना सामान है?" is more natural.
  - "फार्म" here vs "फ़ॉर्म" in Day 1.
  - "जी हाँ इसकी घोषणा की जाएगी।" lacks a comma after जी हाँ (Roman has one).
  - "S-4" (scene 6) vs "S4" (scene 7); extra double space in "S4  है" removed.
  - Speaker labels in scene 7 use visarga "यात्रीः / सहयात्रीः" instead of a colon.
- English fixed: "How many luggage do you have?" → "How many pieces of luggage do you have?"; "form.." → "form."; "yes" → "Yes.".
- Hindi/English mismatches (English kept): d03s1 "किस श्रेणी में?" ("In which class?") vs "Which class would you like to travel in?"; d03s3 "प्लेटफ़ॉर्म संख्या पाँच से" vs "It departs from Platform Number 5"; d03s11 "सीट के पास रखा था" ("I had put it near the seat") vs "I left it near my seat".
- Numbers and money: Hindi keeps ₹650, ₹250, ₹200, 6:30, 9, 32; Roman spells them out (chhah sau pachaas rupaye, saadhe chhah baje, nau, battees). Check TTS on ₹ and digits. Coach "S-4" left as is in Roman.
- Roman to check: Dillee (दिल्ली), Mumbaee, Bhopaal, aarakshan, seaten (सीटें), aakhiree (आख़िरी), voh (वह), minute (मिनट, kept as the English loanword).
