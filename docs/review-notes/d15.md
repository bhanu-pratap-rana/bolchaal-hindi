# Day 15 review notes: Talking with local people

- 5 scenes, 37 lines, 18 feminine variants. Learner role `traveller` (यात्री). Other roles: `local` (स्थानीय व्यक्ति, scene 1 only) and `host` (मेज़बान, scenes 2–5).
- `host` grammar is "m" from "मैं आपकी स्थिति समझ सकता हूँ" (d15s4 line 7).
- `local` grammar is also "m". The speaker gives his name as Amit (मैं अमित हूँ), but none of his verbs shows gender. Please confirm.
- **Learner name:** the learner says "मेरा नाम डेविड है". The feminine variant changes only the verb agreement (आई हूँ), so a woman learner still says "David". The teacher may want a female name in the `f` line.
- d15s3 line 5: the `f` changes two words: "आप अकेली आएँगी". Please check that अकेली is the right form here.
- No `f` was added for "मैं आपका आभारी हूँ" (s2 line 2). आभारी was treated as invariant; some speakers say आभारी for women too, and that is what the line keeps.
- English: "I am from the United States" translates "मैं अमेरिका से आया हूँ" (Roman: America). s2 line 1 has its two English sentences in the reverse order of the Hindi ("Please come in. Welcome!"). This is not an error.
- Roman: हमें = "hamen", हम = "hum", हमारे = "hamaare". The em dash in "करेंगे—चाय या कॉफी" is kept.
- Many glossary items appear only in other forms, or not at all (परिचय देना, स्वीकार करना, मेहमान, क्षमा माँगना, and others). The validator warns about these.
