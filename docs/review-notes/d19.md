# Day 19 review notes: Being a guest in an Indian home

- 1 scene, 27 lines, 7 feminine variants. Learner role `student` (विद्यार्थी). Other roles: `friend` (मित्र, neutral), `mother` (माता, f), `father` (पिता, m) and `family` (परिवार, neutral; one line, "नमस्ते! शुभ यात्रा।").
- **Voice clash, unresolved:** five roles speak in one scene, and there are only four voice slots. `family` shares the mother's voice (female_2) in both variants. The validator warns about this for m and for f. The fix is to split the scene (for example arrival / meal / farewell) or to give the family line to an existing speaker. The teacher should decide.
- Line 1: "...कि आप हमारे घर आए।" has `f` "आईं".
- **Line 5 "नमस्ते बेटा।":** it is unchanged in `f`, because mothers commonly say बेटा to girls too. Some reviewers may prefer बेटी in the woman version.
- Line 3: ये is used for both the parents and the sister. Line 18 has "ये कौन-सा व्यंजन है?", where ये goes with a singular noun; that is colloquial, and the source is kept.
- English line 3 says "this is my sister" for the honorific "ये मेरी बहन हैं".
- Roman: मैंने = "Mainne", आइएगा = "aaiyegaa" (as on day 7), अंकल/आंटी = "uncle/aunty".
